Numerical simulation is important in ocean engineering, but model construction requires expert knowledge. Large language models support automated modelling, but code syntax and successful execution alone do not establish whether generated models and results meet engineering requirements. 原始摘要

Accurate mobile traffic forecasting is crucial for network service management, supporting proactive capacity provisioning, congestion mitigation, and energy-efficient operation. However, many cities and regions are data-scarce (e.g., newly deployed or poorly instrumented areas), thus making traffic prediction a few-shot learning problem and limiting the practicality of standard data-driven models. Existing cross-city solutions primarily rely on data-space alignment to match traffic distributions between data-rich and data-scarce cities, but such alignment is often brittle under real-world distribution shifts, measurement noise, and city-specific bias. To address these limitations, we propose a Conditional Diffusion-based Parameter Generation (CDPG) framework. Instead of aligning raw data, CDPG learns the conditional distribution of high-performing predictor parameters for source cities and directly generates node-specific model parameters for the target city. To support reliable adaptation for network traffic prediction, CDPG conditions parameter generation on multi-modal spatio-temporal context. Specifically, CDPG captures spatial environment cues by combining satellite imagery with large language model (LLM)-derived semantic descriptions, and summarizes complex temporal behaviors via unsupervised pattern clustering. These contextual components guide CDPG in producing parameters tailored to each base station's operational context, thereby improving robustness under cross-city and cross-region shifts. Extensive experiments on three large-scale datasets show that CDPG consistently outperforms state-of-the-art cross-city few-shot baselines by up to 9.72% on standard forecasting metrics and remains robust for few-shot multi-step prediction. 原始摘要

Representative vision-language navigation (VLN) methods typically couple a vision-language model (VLM) brain with a learnable action head and fine-tune the VLM brain on navigation-domain data to predict geometric goals. However, such adaptation can substantially alter base VLM weights and degrade general visual capabilities. We introduce ZeroVLN, a slow-fast navigation framework that achieves effective navigation with zero navigation-domain SFT of the VLM brain. Rather than teaching the brain to predict geometric goals, we equip it with a semantic tool-calling interface for conveying movement decisions to an action expert in natural language. We first adapt the action expert to carry out these movement requests, then jointly improve the brain's movement decisions and the action expert's continuous execution through closed-loop reinforcement learning (RL). Trained on the same navigation episodes, ZeroVLN substantially outperforms representative SFT-based hierarchical VLN methods on standard VLN-CE (continuous environments) benchmarks, while inducing only minor changes to the brain's weights and largely preserving its general capabilities. To our knowledge, ZeroVLN is the first slow-fast VLN framework with zero navigation-domain SFT of the VLM brain, and the first to jointly optimize its slow and fast policies through closed-loop agentic RL in VLN-CE.

原始摘要

Machine learning (ML) is now a standard tool for forecasting the output of solar photovoltaic (PV) plants, yet published accuracy figures are often hard to compare and harder to trust. Many studies validate with random splits, report no reference forecast, and present feature attributions without saying what they can and cannot show. This paper reviews the main families of ML methods for PV power forecasting and resource assessment, from tree ensembles to deep and foundation models, and sets out a reproducible workflow that combines automated model search with explainable AI. The workflow is then tested on an openly available dataset: a 16.32 kWp plant in Porto, Portugal, forecast one day ahead from a 4 km numerical weather prediction grid. Over a full test year, the best models reach a normalised root mean square error (nRMSE) of 11.6% of installed capacity, about half the error of smart persistence. The choice of input features changed accuracy far more than the choice among strong algorithms, and shuffled cross-validation was mildly optimistic. After conformal calibration, the nominal 80% prediction intervals covered 81.7% of observations, against 64.5% before. SHAP rankings stayed stable when the model was retrained on different periods but shifted when correlated inputs were removed, so attributions should be read as properties of a model rather than of the plant. The paper closes with practical lessons and open problems, including deployment on edge hardware and the effect of a changing climate.
